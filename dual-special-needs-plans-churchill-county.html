Dual Special Needs Plans in Churchill County, Nevada — Who Qualifies and What to Check

If you have both Medicare and Medicaid in Churchill County, a D-SNP can wrap your coverage into one plan. Here is how it works locally — including every city and ZIP code in the county.

Churchill County runs nearly 5,000 square miles, and essentially all of its medical care sits in one town. That single fact should drive how you evaluate a Dual Special Needs Plan here. Plan extras like an over-the-counter allowance are worth having, but they matter far less than whether the plan contracts with the hospital and clinics in Fallon.

A Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (D-SNP) is a Medicare Advantage plan for people who have both Medicare and Medicaid. It combines Part A, Part B and Part D prescription coverage in one plan, usually at a $0 premium, because Medicaid already covers most Medicare cost-sharing. Nevada runs a coordination-only D-SNP program, and whether you qualify in Churchill County depends on which dual eligibility category you fall into. Eligibility categories, how Nevada structures its program, and the enrollment windows are covered in full on the Nevada D-SNP guide →.

Getting care in Churchill County

Banner Churchill Community Hospital in Fallon is the county's hospital, and the Fallon Tribal Health Center serves members of the Fallon Paiute-Shoshone Tribe.

Because Fallon is the only real medical hub for a very large county, confirming that Banner Churchill and its affiliated clinics are in network matters more here than any list of supplemental benefits. Ask us to verify the hospital first, then compare extras.

Naval Air Station Fallon sits just outside town, so a share of the county's Medicare population also carries TRICARE For Life or VA benefits layered on top of Medicare and Medicaid — a combination worth reviewing carefully before changing plans.

Before You Enroll

Three things to check in Churchill County

These are the questions that actually change the answer here.

  • Verify Banner Churchill first. It is the county's hospital. A plan that does not contract with it is usually the wrong plan in Churchill County, whatever else it offers.
  • Sort out VA or TRICARE overlap before enrolling. With NAS Fallon nearby, many local Medicare beneficiaries also carry VA or TRICARE For Life. Those interact with a D-SNP in ways worth mapping out first.
  • Ask about the drive to Reno. Specialty referrals leave the county. Check the plan's Washoe County network and what it pays toward getting you there.

More plan choice than these counties are used to

Rural Nevada has historically been thin ground for Medicare Advantage. Counties in this part of the state have long had far fewer plans than Clark or Washoe — sometimes only a handful, sometimes nothing worth switching for. That picture has changed for 2026, and Churchill County is one of the counties where it is worth a fresh look.

If you priced Medicare Advantage in Churchill County a few years ago and concluded there was nothing here for you, that conclusion may simply be out of date. This is the part we would rather walk through with you directly — plan availability is defined county by county and changes every year, so the only answer that means anything is the one for your ZIP code, this plan year.

And Medicaid changed too, on January 1, 2026

Churchill County was part of the roughly 75,000 rural Nevadans moved out of fee-for-service Medicaid on January 1, 2026. For Fallon residents the practical question is whether your new Medicaid managed care plan and your Medicare coverage both work cleanly with Banner Churchill — that is the pairing that determines how smooth your care actually is.
