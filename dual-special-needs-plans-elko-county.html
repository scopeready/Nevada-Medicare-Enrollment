Dual Special Needs Plans in Elko County, Nevada — Who Qualifies and What to Check

If you have both Medicare and Medicaid in Elko County, a D-SNP can wrap your coverage into one plan. Here is how it works locally — including every city and ZIP code in the county.

Elko County is roughly 17,200 square miles — larger than several states. For someone with both Medicare and Medicaid living in Montello, Jarbidge or Owyhee, the practical barrier to care is not what a plan covers, it is the distance to reach it. That makes transportation benefits, mail-order pharmacy and telehealth coverage the parts of a Dual Special Needs Plan worth reading most closely here.

A Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (D-SNP) is a Medicare Advantage plan for people who have both Medicare and Medicaid. It combines Part A, Part B and Part D prescription coverage in one plan, usually at a $0 premium, because Medicaid already covers most Medicare cost-sharing. Nevada runs a coordination-only D-SNP program, and whether you qualify in Elko County depends on which dual eligibility category you fall into. Eligibility categories, how Nevada structures its program, and the enrollment windows are covered in full on the Nevada D-SNP guide →.

Getting care in Elko County

Northeastern Nevada Regional Hospital in Elko is the referral hospital for the whole region. The Owyhee Community Health Facility serves the Duck Valley Reservation in the far north of the county.

In a county this size, a plan's routine transportation benefit is not a perk — for many residents it is the difference between keeping an appointment and skipping it. Ask how many one-way trips a plan allows per year and whether the distances involved actually qualify.

At roughly 17,200 square miles, Elko County is one of the largest counties in the United States by area. Driving from Jackpot on the Idaho line to Elko is a substantial trip, and reaching Montello or Jarbidge is longer still.

Before You Enroll

Three things to check in Elko County

These are the questions that actually change the answer here.

  • Confirm Northeastern Nevada Regional Hospital. It is the referral hospital for the region. Start there before comparing anything else.
  • Read the transportation benefit closely. Ask how many one-way trips per year are covered and whether Elko County distances actually qualify. Some allowances are capped by mileage.
  • If you are near a state line, ask about it. West Wendover residents commonly use Salt Lake City providers, and Jackpot sits on the Idaho line. Out-of-state network coverage varies by plan.

More plan choice than these counties are used to

Rural Nevada has historically been thin ground for Medicare Advantage. Counties in this part of the state have long had far fewer plans than Clark or Washoe — sometimes only a handful, sometimes nothing worth switching for. That picture has changed for 2026, and Elko County is one of the counties where it is worth a fresh look.

If you priced Medicare Advantage in Elko County a few years ago and concluded there was nothing here for you, that conclusion may simply be out of date. And Medicaid changed too, on January 1, 2026

Elko County joined Medicaid managed care on January 1, 2026. In a county this size the change matters most for the services that involve travel — non-emergency medical transportation was one of the areas the state specifically asked the new rural plans to improve, and it is worth finding out what you are now entitled to.
