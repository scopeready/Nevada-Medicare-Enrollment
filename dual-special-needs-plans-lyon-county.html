Dual Special Needs Plans in Lyon County, Nevada — Who Qualifies and What to Check

If you have both Medicare and Medicaid in Lyon County, a D-SNP can wrap your coverage into one plan. Here is how it works locally — including every city and ZIP code in the county.

Lyon County is the most populated county in this D-SNP service area and one of the fastest growing in Nevada — and it is the one county here where your ZIP code genuinely changes the answer. Yerington, Dayton and Fernley pull toward three different hospital systems. The best Dual Special Needs Plan in Fernley is not automatically the best one in Smith Valley.

A Dual Eligible Special Needs Plan (D-SNP) is a Medicare Advantage plan for people who have both Medicare and Medicaid. It combines Part A, Part B and Part D prescription coverage in one plan, usually at a $0 premium, because Medicaid already covers most Medicare cost-sharing. Nevada runs a coordination-only D-SNP program, and whether you qualify in Lyon County depends on which dual eligibility category you fall into. Eligibility categories, how Nevada structures its program, and the enrollment windows are covered in full on the Nevada D-SNP guide →.

Getting care in Lyon County

South Lyon Medical Center in Yerington serves Mason Valley and Smith Valley. Residents in Dayton and Fernley more often use Carson Tahoe in Carson City or the Reno–Sparks hospitals, and Silver Springs sits close to Banner Churchill in Fallon.

Because the county splits across at least three hospital markets, comparing plans at the county level is not good enough in Lyon County. Give us your ZIP code and your actual providers, and we will compare on that basis — it changes the shortlist substantially.

Lyon County spans the I-80 corridor at Fernley, the US-50 corridor through Dayton and Silver Springs, and the agricultural Mason and Smith valleys in the south — roughly 64,000 residents across about 2,000 square miles.

Before You Enroll

Three things to check in Lyon County

These are the questions that actually change the answer here.

  • Compare by ZIP code, not by county. Yerington, Dayton, Fernley and Silver Springs pull toward four different care markets. County-level comparison is not precise enough here.
  • If you are in Fernley or Dayton, check the Reno network. Most specialty care from the north of the county happens in Washoe County, even though you live in Lyon.
  • If you are in Mason or Smith Valley, check South Lyon Medical Center. It is the local hospital for the south of the county and should be your starting point.

More plan choice than these counties are used to

Rural Nevada has historically been thin ground for Medicare Advantage. Counties in this part of the state have long had far fewer plans than Clark or Washoe — sometimes only a handful, sometimes nothing worth switching for. That picture has changed for 2026, and Lyon County is one of the counties where it is worth a fresh look.

If you priced Medicare Advantage in Lyon County a few years ago and concluded there was nothing here for you, that conclusion may simply be out of date. This is the part we would rather walk through with you directly — plan availability is defined county by county and changes every year, so the only answer that means anything is the one for your ZIP code, this plan year.

And Medicaid changed too, on January 1, 2026

Lyon County joined Medicaid managed care on January 1, 2026. With the county spanning three different hospital markets, the new plan's network in Carson City, Reno–Sparks and Fallon may matter more than its network in Yerington, depending on where in the county you live.
